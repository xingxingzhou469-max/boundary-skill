# Source map / 资料起点

**Link check: 2026-10-05.** The links below are public starting points, not a pre-approved source list or permanent whitelist. Each linked page was opened on the check date; that verifies access to the page, not any claim in it. A landing page, search result, abstract, or repository homepage is not evidence for a card or report. Open the particular paper, dataset, legal instrument, archive item, or textbook section; read the relevant passage and its date, method, scope, and limitations. Record the exact item URL and what it supports. Recheck access and suitability when researching; prefer independent publishers for independent evidence. Public access does not imply permission to reproduce a source's full text or images.

Europeana is an aggregator: follow the item to its contributing institution and inspect provenance, date, and rights. For contested history, pair records with relevant scholarship and primary sources. |

## Portable research practices from open-source projects

These are workflow ideas adapted for a plain skill, not claims that either project guarantees accurate output. Both project READMEs were opened on 2026-10-05: [STORM](https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm#readme) and [GPT Researcher](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher#readme).

1. **Turn the central question into answerable subquestions and check perspective gaps.** STORM describes perspective-guided questions and follow-up questions grounded in source material; its Co-STORM README also describes surfacing information not yet used in the discussion. A skill can ask what evidence, population, time period, or viewpoint is missing, without pretending to convene experts. See the [STORM README's research approach](https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm#storm) and [Co-STORM overview](https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm#co-storm).
2. **Research and outline before drafting.** STORM documents separate steps for collecting knowledge, organizing it as an outline, drafting from the outline and collected material, and polishing. For Boundary, a short outline can map each section to the question it answers and the sources that support it; draft only after gaps are visible. This is a workflow sequence, not a reason to make a daily card long. See the [STORM module overview](https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm#customization-of-the-pipeline).
3. **Keep a compact claim-to-source ledger and run a counter-evidence pass.** GPT Researcher describes generating research questions, summarizing and source-tracking each resource, then filtering and aggregating the findings; its README also warns that selective source pools can introduce bias. A lightweight skill adaptation is a private list of central claim → exact source → what it supports → date/scope limit, followed by “what credible evidence would narrow or contradict this?” Do not manufacture balance when the evidence is one-sided. See [GPT Researcher's process](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher#architecture) and [its source-bias note](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher#why-gpt-researcher).

Do not import the projects' crawler, agent orchestration, provider/API keys, or paid-service assumptions into a pure skill. Their READMEs document software pipelines and dependencies, not an independent validation that generated reports are correct; every retained source and claim still needs direct review.
