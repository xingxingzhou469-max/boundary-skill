# Source and evidence policy

## Rules for every card

1. Research before drafting; do not rely on model memory alone.
2. Open and read the underlying pages, papers, or records. Search snippets are not evidence.
3. Use at least two independent reliable sources for every daily card.
4. Prefer primary and authoritative sources. Use strong secondary sources to explain context.
5. Verify that two sources are genuinely independent, not copies of the same press release or claim.
6. Place links near the claims they support and include a short source list.
7. If adequate evidence is unavailable, reject the topic and select another.
8. Never invent a citation, DOI, author, date, quotation, or scholarly consensus.
9. State uncertainty and disagreement in plain language.
10. Record access/publication dates for facts likely to change.

For every retained source, record its original title, URL, source kind, publisher or institution, and the specific claim it supports. `publisher` identifies the independent organization responsible for the source; do not invent different publisher labels for material derived from the same underlying organization.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order appropriate to the subject:

1. Original research, primary documents, official datasets, laws, standards, archival material, museum or collection records.
2. Systematic reviews, scholarly syntheses, clinical guidelines, regulator publications, authoritative reference works.
3. Expert secondary analysis from universities, research institutes, major museums, professional bodies, and reputable publishers.
4. General journalism only for context or recent events, not as the sole support for technical or disputed claims.

Wikipedia may help discover terminology and sources but should not be one of the final two supporting sources when stronger material is available.

## Academic topics

- Prefer peer-reviewed papers and field-level reviews.
- Identify whether evidence is a review, experiment, observational study, model, or scholarly interpretation.
- Include methods and limitations in research briefs and full reports.
- Do not imply that one paper establishes consensus.
- Provide DOI or stable paper links when available.

## Medical topics

- Prefer current systematic reviews, clinical guidelines, public-health agencies, and regulator information.
- Distinguish laboratory, animal, observational, and randomized human evidence.
- State applicable population, important harms, contraindications, and regulatory status when relevant.
- Do not diagnose the user or tailor treatment to personal symptoms.
- Make clear that educational content does not replace qualified medical care.

## Financial and economic topics

- Prefer regulator filings, central banks, statistical agencies, audited reports, and well-defined academic evidence.
- State the data date, jurisdiction, assumptions, and material counter-evidence.
- Separate historical explanation from forecasts.
- Do not issue personalized recommendations or direct buy/sell instructions.

## Legal topics

- State jurisdiction and effective date.
- Prefer legislation, court decisions, regulators, and official guidance.
- Distinguish a general explanation from legal advice.
- Do not assume that one jurisdiction's rule applies elsewhere.

## History and contested topics

- Distinguish primary evidence, later tradition, and modern interpretation.
- Avoid turning uncertain attribution into a clean hero story.
- Name major disputes and why the evidence permits more than one reading.
- For people traditionally called a sole “discoverer,” check collaborators, predecessors, and excluded contributors.

## Research brief and full report quality

- Support each central conclusion with citations at the point of use.
- Use at least three strong, genuinely independent sources for a small research brief.
- For a full report, choose sources according to the coverage required by its specific research question rather than meeting a fixed source count.
- Include evidence strength, counter-evidence, limitations, and unanswered questions.
- End with complete, usable references; no placeholders.
